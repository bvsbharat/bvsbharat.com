03
A note

Geography and culture

Assam is about 78,400 square kilometres: the wide Brahmaputra valley, the smaller Barak valley in the south, and two hill districts — Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao. Nearly two-fifths of the state can flood. That is not a disaster footnote. Flood is how this land was made, and how Kaziranga still works.

People here call the place Axom. The old phrase is “the land of the red river and the blue hills.” Assamese is the link language; Bodo, Mishing, Karbi, Dimasa and many others are spoken too, and so are the languages of the tea-garden communities. Hindu Vaishnavite satras — monasteries of Sankardev’s faith — sit beside namghars, mosques, churches and indigenous shrines. A xorai (bell-metal offering stand) and a gamosa (the red-and-white towel) are the two objects you will see again and again.

Food is lighter than much of India: rice, fish, greens, a sour note (tenga) and an alkaline one (khar). Hospitality is a greeting — bhaat khaisa ne? means have you eaten? Accept tea when it is offered. Fold your hands for নমস্কাৰ.

The red river · Luit

Brahmaputra

It is born as the Yarlung Tsangpo in Tibet, becomes the Siang or Dihang in Arunachal, the Brahmaputra in Assam, and the Jamuna in Bangladesh. About 2,900 kilometres from glacier to Bay of Bengal. In Assam it is a moving country: sandbars, chars, and Majuli, one of the world’s great river islands.

The water often looks pale with silt — hence “red river.” It feeds the beels of Kaziranga, the dolphins of the river, the paddy, and the fear of June. From the Tezpur side you may cross on the Kolia Bhomora bridge. Watch how wide it is. That width is the story.

National parks

Assam’s official list has seven national parks. You are going to one of them. Nameri is the neighbour of Durrung, if a spare morning appears.

KazirangaUNESCO. One-horned rhino, elephant, wild buffalo, swamp deer, tiger. Your safari. ManasUNESCO. Himalayan foothills, west. Tiger, pygmy hog, golden langur. NameriSonitpur, by Arunachal. Jia Bharali rafting, hornbill, tiger. Close to the estate. OrangNorth bank. A smaller rhino landscape, sometimes called a mini-Kaziranga. Dibru-SaikhowaRiverine, east. Maguri beel, feral horses, winter birds. Dehing PatkaiRainforest of upper Assam, notified 2021. Hoolock, hollong trees. RaimonaWestern Assam, 2021. Golden langur country, next to Bhutan.

Ranges at Kaziranga: Kohora (central), Bagori (west, often best for rhino), Agaratoli (east, birds), Burapahar (hills, gibbon).

If a spare morning from Durrung

Nameri is the close one — Jia Bharali, hornbills, a different forest from Kaziranga’s grass. Orang is a smaller rhino park on the north bank. Ask the estate; do not freelance a gate.

In any park: muted clothes, no playback of animal calls, no litter, no getting down where you were not asked. The jeep is a hide, not a throne.

Living things

Flora and fauna

This is monsoon forest, flood grassland, river, and tea. Kaziranga’s “big five” are the greater one-horned rhinoceros, Asian elephant, wild water buffalo, swamp deer (barasingha) and tiger. The western hoolock gibbon — India’s only ape — sings from canopy south of the Brahmaputra. Gangetic dolphins still roll in the river. In November, migratory waterbirds arrive on the beels.

Cloth of the valley

Assamese handloom

Assam weaves three silks. Muga is the golden one, from the moth Antheraea assamensis, almost unique to this valley — it deepens with wear. Eri (endi) is the warm, wool-like silk; the moth is allowed to leave the cocoon, so it is often called ahimsa silk. Pat is the white mulberry silk. Sualkuchi, on the north bank near Guwahati, is the weaving town.

The woman’s dress is the mekhela chador — a wrap skirt and a drape — often in muga or pat with red geometric borders. The gamosa, white cotton with a red woven end, is towel, gift, honour, and flag of feeling. The jaapi is the wide bamboo hat of the fields. A gamosa offered to you is not a souvenir first. It is a welcome.

The year in three Bihus

Annual festivals

Rongali / BohagMid-April. Assamese new year. The great one: dance, dhol, pepa (buffalo horn), new clothes, the spring. Kati / KongaliMid-October. Quiet, lamps in the paddy (saki). You have just missed it in November. Magh / BhogaliMid-January. Harvest feast, community fire (meji), uruka night, feasting.

Other days you may hear of: Ambubachi Mela at Kamakhya in June; Raas at the satras of Majuli in autumn; Ali-Aye-Ligang of the Mishing in February. Bihu is not only a performance. It is the calendar the valley still keeps.

Camellia sinensis var. assamica

About tea

Left alone, tea is a tree. In Assam it is trained as a low bush with a flat plucking table. The harvest is the young shoot: two leaves and a bud. Durrung has done this since 1864, across 1,400 acres.

PruningDecember–January, when the bush is dormant. A 2–4 year cycle in the plains. Between prunes, a light cut called skiffing keeps the table. HarvestPlucking rounds every week or so, March to November. Fine plucking = two leaves and a bud. Coarser plucking makes stronger, rougher tea. FlushesFirst flush (Mar–Apr) is lighter. Second flush (May–Jun) is the famous malty Assam, often with golden tips. Then rains, then autumn. NovemberLate season. Some sections still plucked; others heading toward prune. Ask what the bushes are doing this week.
Types you may be poured

Orthodox black — whole or broken leaf, rolled, oxidised; aroma and malt. CTC — crush, tear, curl; strong, brisk, the everyday chai leaf. Green / white / oolong — less oxidation; some estates now make them for tasting. Second-flush golden tips — the prize cup if they have it.

Factory steps, orthodox: wither → roll → oxidise → dry → sort. CTC skips the gentle roll.
